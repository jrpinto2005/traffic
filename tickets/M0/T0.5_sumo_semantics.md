# T0.5 — Confirm SUMO semantics behind D-025 and D-022

**Archetype:** Spike
**Milestone:** M0
**Depends on:** T0.2
**Implements decisions:** confirms or amends `D-025`, `D-022`

## Context
Two settled decisions rest on assumptions about what SUMO actually does. `D-025` defines a
queue in terms of halting, and assumes SUMO's native halting counters use a 0.1 m/s threshold
without a duration condition. `D-022` assumes `jtrrouter` provides turn-ratio routing with
declared sink edges, and that mid-link `arrivalPos` removal is distinguishable in trip output
from vehicles that never finished.

If either assumption is wrong, the decision is amended — that is the normal outcome of a
spike, not a failure.

## Questions

**Queue semantics (`D-025`)**
1. What exactly does `getLastStepHaltingNumber` measure — which speed threshold, and does it
   apply any duration condition?
2. Do lane-area detectors report the same thing, or something different?
3. Can we get, or must we compute, the "halting continuously for ≥ 1 s" condition `D-025`
   requires?
4. How is queue **length in metres** obtained, and does it account for vehicle length and
   gaps?

**Demand semantics (`D-022`)**
5. Does `jtrrouter` do what we assume — turn ratios per (junction, from-edge), declared sink
   edges, a maximum-edges limit for the memoryless-routing artifact?
6. Does `<flow>` with `period="exp(rate)"` produce genuine Poisson arrivals? Confirm
   empirically, do not assume.
7. Does `departPos` at an interior position work for mid-link sources?
8. **Critical:** when a vehicle is removed at a mid-link `arrivalPos`, how does it appear in
   `tripinfo` output? Is it distinguishable from a vehicle that reached a boundary sink, and
   from one that never finished? `07_EXPERIMENT_METHODOLOGY.md` §4's three-way accounting
   depends entirely on this being separable.

## Deliverable
`research/M0_sumo_semantics.md`: a direct answer to each question with the evidence, and an
explicit statement per decision — **confirmed as written**, or **amend as follows**.

## Non-goals
1. Do NOT implement metrics or the demand generator. Those are T1.8 and T1.5.
2. Do NOT design around a problem you find. Report it; amending the decision is the
   architect's call.

## Escalate if
1. Question 8 has no clean answer — the three-way accounting is load-bearing and an
   alternative mechanism would be needed.
2. `jtrrouter` cannot express per-edge attrition, requiring a different realisation of
   `D-022`.
