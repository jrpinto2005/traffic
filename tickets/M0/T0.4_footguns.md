# T0.4 — Reproduce the three validity footguns

**Archetype:** Spike
**Milestone:** M0
**Depends on:** T0.2
**Implements decisions:** confirms `D-008`

## Context
**This is the highest value-per-hour ticket in M0.** `01_CRITICAL_REVIEW.md` G-6 identifies
three SUMO behaviours that silently invalidate experiments, none of which appear anywhere in
the original specification. The most dangerous is teleporting: SUMO relocates stuck vehicles
after 300 s by default, which under congestion deletes the very congestion being measured —
and deletes more of it for worse controllers. A default-settings comparison can therefore
rank an excellent controller below a terrible one with no visible error.

The purpose here is to see each failure happen on purpose, so it is recognisable later.

## Questions
1. **Teleporting.** Drive the T0.2 scenario above capacity with default settings. How many
   teleports occur, how do they appear in the output, and how much do the headline metrics
   differ between `--time-to-teleport 300` (default) and `-1` (disabled)?
2. **Insertion loss.** With demand above what the network can accept, how many vehicles fail
   to insert, how is that reported, and how far does applied demand drift from configured
   demand?
3. **Gridlock.** Can the scenario be driven into full gridlock? What does it look like in the
   output, and what is the cheapest reliable way to detect it programmatically?

## Deliverable
`research/M0_footguns.md` with, per footgun: how it was induced, its **signature in the
output**, the magnitude of the metric distortion it causes, and the exact detection rule
T1.8 should implement.

The metric-distortion number matters most. "Teleporting changed mean time loss by X%" is what
makes `D-008` persuasive to a future reader — including us.

## Non-goals
1. Do NOT implement the detection logic in production code. That is T1.8.
2. Do NOT fix anything. Reproducing is the deliverable.
3. Do NOT test controllers. Fixed-time throughout.

## Escalate if
1. Teleporting cannot be disabled, or `-1` behaves unexpectedly.
2. A fourth failure mode of the same character appears — that is a finding, and `D-008`
   should grow.
